GROUP 1 · MEDICAL TECHNOLOGY
Biosensor Engineering & Device Prototyping
Four sensing platforms, one biomarker. Students detect the same target four different ways, then take the best platform through reuse testing, CAD design, a live web readout, and two wearable devices.
Lab 1  QCM Biosensor & Biomarker Detection   (40 hours)
Mass-Sensitive Detection on a Quartz Crystal
Start with the platform Surazense built its research on. Students calibrate a Quartz Crystal Microbalance, sweep 8–12 MHz through an ESP32 readout, functionalize the gold surface, then watch a biomarker bind in real time and decide whether the target is present.
Key skills taught:
Frequency sweep & resonance tracking
Gold surface functionalization
Reading a real binding curve
Lab 2  SAW Biosensor & Biomarker Detection   (40 hours)
Surface Acoustic Wave Sensing
Same biomarker, different physics. Here the wave travels across the surface instead of through the bulk crystal. Students drive a SAW device, record phase and amplitude as the target binds, and compare sensitivity directly against their Lab 1 result.
Key skills taught:
SAW device operation
Phase vs amplitude readout
Cross-platform sensitivity comparison
Lab 3  FET Biosensor & Biomarker Detection   (40 hours)
Charge-Based Detection & Screening Effects
Detect a molecule by the charge it carries rather than the mass it adds. Students bias a field-effect transistor biosensor, track how the transfer curve shifts as the target binds, and test how buffer ionic strength changes what the sensor can still see.
Key skills taught:
FET transfer-curve measurement
Gate bias & threshold shift
Ionic strength (Debye) effects
Lab 4  Portable Electrochemical Sensor & Biomarker Detection   (40 hours)
Resistance Readout on Screen-Printed Electrodes
Build a detector that fits in a pocket. Students assemble a screen-printed electrode cell and read one quantity only — electrical resistance — as the biomarker binds to the electrode surface. Rising resistance means more target captured, and their own calibration curve turns ohms into a concentration.
Key skills taught:
Screen-printed electrode setup
Resistance readout & baseline stability
Calibration curve & detection limit
Lab 5  Sensor Regeneration, Reuse & DNA Dehybridization   (40 hours)
How Many Times Can One Chip Be Used?
Ask the question that decides whether a test kit is disposable or reusable. Students hybridize DNA onto the sensor, strip it off again by raising temperature and changing buffer, then repeat the cycle and measure how much signal survives each round.
Key skills taught:
DNA hybridization & dehybridization
Temperature and buffer control
Cycle reproducibility (%CV)
Lab 6  Biosensor Modeling & Simulation with CAD   (40 hours)
Engineering Design Before Fabrication
Design the sensor before building it. Students draw electrode and flow-cell geometry in CAD, simulate how mass loading and liquid flow affect the signal, sweep dimensions to find what improves sensitivity, and export a file ready for 3D printing.
Key skills taught:
CAD electrode & flow-cell design
Mass-loading and flow simulation
Parameter sweep & design optimization
Lab 7  Bio-Design Website with AI   (40 hours)
Publishing Live Sensor Data
Turn a working sensor into something other people can use. Students build a web dashboard that receives live readings from their device, connect an AI assistant that explains a result in plain language, and publish the page for their school to try.
Key skills taught:
Live data dashboard build
AI assistant integration via API
Publishing & user testing
Lab 8  Wearable Vital-Sign Sensing   (40 hours)
PPG, Sampling Rate & Motion Artifacts
Build a working heart-rate monitor, then find out why it fails. Students wire a PPG sensor to an ESP32, choose a sampling rate, and separate a real pulse from the artifacts that appear the moment the wearer moves.
Key skills taught:
PPG signal acquisition
Sampling rate selection
Motion artifact rejection
Lab 9  Smart Insole & Fall Detection   (40 hours)
Fall Detection & Gait Analysis Technology
Learn the technology behind fall detection and pressure-based gait analysis. Students read pressure distribution across the foot from a sensing insole, follow how an accelerometer signal separates a real fall from ordinary movement, and interpret gait from their own recorded walks — stride timing, weight transfer, and left–right balance.
Key skills taught:
Reading a foot pressure map
Fall detection from accelerometer signals
Gait pattern & balance interpretation
GROUP 2 · MEDICAL & BIOMEDICAL SCIENCES
Wet Lab Foundations
The bench skills every research project assumes. Start here if students have never worked in a controlled laboratory.
Lab 10  Lab Safety, Pipetting & Solution Prep   (20 hours)
Prerequisite for All Wet Labs
Enter the lab safely and measure accurately. Students cover BSL-1/2 practice and waste handling, verify micropipette accuracy by mass, and prepare buffers from molarity calculations and serial dilutions.
Key skills taught:
PPE and waste protocols
Micropipette accuracy checks
Molarity & serial dilution
Lab 11  PCR & Gel Electrophoresis   (20 hours)
Amplify, Separate, Interpret
Amplify a target sequence and read the result. Students set up reactions and thermal cycling, cast and run an agarose gel, then size bands against a ladder and judge whether the result is trustworthy.
Key skills taught:
Reaction setup & thermal cycling
Agarose gel casting and running
Band sizing & contamination controls
Lab 12  Protein Quantification & ELISA   (20 hours)
Standard Curves & Detection Limits
Turn color into a number. Students build a protein standard curve, run a sandwich ELISA on a microplate, read absorbance, and calculate the assay's limit of detection from their own blanks.
Key skills taught:
Standard curve construction
Sandwich ELISA workflow
Limit of detection calculation
Lab 13  Microscopy & Cell Viability   (20 hours)
Counting, Staining, Judging Health
See the sample instead of only measuring it. Students prepare stained slides, count cells on a hemocytometer, and use trypan blue exclusion to report viability with a defensible number.
Key skills taught:
Slide preparation & staining
Hemocytometer counting
Trypan blue viability assay

THEORY · CLINICAL CONTEXT · METHODOLOGY
Scientific Foundations & Research Methodology
Courses 1–4 explain why the instruments in Part 1 work and what clinical question they answer. Courses 5–8 turn a student's measurements into a defensible piece of research, and Course 9 is delivered by the Suratec engineering team.
Course 1  Principles of Biosensing & Transduction   (3 hours)
Acoustic, FET & Electrochemical Platforms
Understand how a molecular event becomes an electrical signal — across all four platforms used in Labs 1 to 4. Covers the Sauerbrey relation, charge screening in solution, charge-transfer resistance, and how sensitivity, selectivity and limit of detection are defined and compared.
Key skills taught:
Four transduction families compared
Sauerbrey relation & Debye length
Sensitivity, selectivity & LOD
Course 2  Molecular Diagnostics Fundamentals   (3 hours)
Biomarkers, Targets & Test Performance
Learn what a diagnostic test actually claims. Students work through biomarker classes — protein, nucleic acid, metabolite — how a target and sample type are chosen, and how sensitivity, specificity and predictive value are calculated from a real 2×2 result table.
Key skills taught:
Biomarker classes & sample types
Target selection criteria
Sensitivity, specificity & predictive value
Course 3  Basic Translational Medicine   (3 hours)
From Bench to Bedside
Follow an idea through the stages that decide whether it ever reaches a patient: preclinical evidence, prototype validation, clinical study phases, regulatory approval and cost. Case studies show where promising sensors stop, and why that is usually not a science problem.
Key skills taught:
Translational stages T0–T4
Prototype validation requirements
Regulatory, cost & adoption barriers
Course 4  Liquid Biopsy & Cancer Screening   (3 hours)
Cell-Free DNA, RNA & Population Screening
The clinical case behind Labs 1 to 5. Covers cell-free DNA and RNA in circulation, why a single blood draw can carry tumour information, HPV and cervical cancer screening as a worked example, and what a screening programme must prove before a country adopts it.
Key skills taught:
cfDNA/cfRNA biology
HPV screening case study
Evaluating a screening programme
Course 5  Literature Search & Screening   (3 hours)
PubMed, Scopus & Reference Management
Find out what is already known before spending a single reagent. Students build Boolean queries in PubMed and Scopus, screen abstracts against their own inclusion criteria, and keep a citation library that stays clean to the end of the project.
Key skills taught:
Boolean & MeSH search strategy
Abstract screening criteria
Reference manager workflow
Course 6  Research Question & Proposal Design   (3 hours)
Hypothesis, Variables, Controls
Turn curiosity into a workable plan. Students narrow a broad interest into a testable hypothesis, define variables and the controls that make a result believable, and write a one-page proposal with a realistic timeline and materials list.
Key skills taught:
Framing a testable hypothesis
Variable & control definition
One-page proposal writing
Course 7  Research Data Analysis in Python   (4 hours)
From Raw Sensor Log to Reportable Figure
Take the sensor logs recorded in Part 1 and analyze them properly. Students clean data with pandas, apply baseline correction and smoothing, fit curves, add error bars, and produce figures that hold up in a competition or a manuscript.
Key skills taught:
Data cleaning with pandas
Baseline correction, smoothing & curve fitting
Error bars & publication-quality plots
Course 8  AI for Research Projects   (4 hours)
Using AI Honestly and Effectively
Learn where AI genuinely helps a student research project and where it produces confident errors. Students use AI for literature triage, code assistance and figure drafting, verify every claim against a real source, and follow the disclosure rules competitions and journals now expect.
Key skills taught:
Prompting for research tasks
Verifying AI output against sources
Disclosure & academic integrity rules
Course 9  Gait Analysis & Pressure Sensor Technology   (3 hours)
Delivered by the Suratec Engineering Team
The engineers who build Suratec's pressure-sensing products explain how a measurement of walking becomes clinical information. Covers pressure sensor types and how they are calibrated, the phases of the gait cycle, what a pressure map reveals about fall risk and diabetic foot risk, and how the data reaches a clinician.
Key skills taught:
Pressure sensor types & calibration
Gait cycle phases & pressure maps
Clinical use in fall and diabetic foot risk
